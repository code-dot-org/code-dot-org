# Security Policy

CodeAI welcomes reports from security researchers and the public. If you
believe you have found a vulnerability, a privacy issue, exposed data or
another security problem in this repository or the sites it runs, we want
to hear from you.

This file summarizes our Vulnerability Disclosure Policy. The policy itself,
including the current status of our bug bounty program, is published at
https://code.org/security and governs where the two differ.

## Reporting a vulnerability

Email security@code.org. Do not open a public issue, discussion or pull
request for a vulnerability, and do not discuss it in public before it is
resolved.

Include as much of the following as you can:

- the affected target and URL, or the file and line in this repository;
- a description of the vulnerability and its potential impact;
- clear reproduction steps and a minimal proof of concept;
- the date and environment in which you tested;
- relevant screenshots, logs, or request and response details.

The more detail you give, the faster we can triage and fix the issue. If
you are unsure whether your research is within this policy, email
security@code.org before going further.

Bounty eligibility, if the paid program is open, is decided under the
program's own terms; see https://code.org/security.

## Scope

You may report a suspected vulnerability in any asset owned or operated by
CodeAI. You may actively test only the targets listed as in scope in our
Bugcrowd engagement brief, https://bugcrowd.com/engagements/codeorg, and
only under the testing rules published there. Testing any other asset
requires our prior written authorization.

This policy does not cover assets that CodeAI does not own. Report
vulnerabilities in third-party systems to their owners.

## What we ask of you

- Report vulnerabilities to us promptly.
- Do not violate anyone's privacy, disrupt our services, destroy data, or
  degrade the experience of students, teachers or other users.
- Use only accounts you own, or accounts whose owner has given you
  explicit permission. Students are minors; never use or target their
  accounts.
- If you reach data that is not yours, access only the minimum needed to
  demonstrate the issue. Stop and report immediately if you encounter
  personal information, student project data, or other users' data.
- Do not test where testing could cause disruption or data loss, and do
  not use denial of service, spam, social engineering or physical attacks.
- Give us at least 180 days from your initial report to resolve the issue
  before any public disclosure.
- Do not engage in extortion.

## What you can expect from us

- We will respond to your report and work with you to understand and
  validate it.
- We will try to keep you informed as we review it.
- We will work to fix confirmed vulnerabilities in a timely manner.
- We extend Safe Harbor to research conducted under this policy, as set out
  at https://code.org/security: we will not pursue legal action for
  good-faith research that follows it.
