# 05 — Parent received a permission email

**Verdict: PARTLY**

## Path taken
- CodeAI Documentation (home page) — 0 clicks (start)
- Permission request from CodeAI (Students > Parents and guardians, sidebar) — 1 click

## Answering sentence
"You received this email because your child created a CodeAI account and, under your state's children's privacy laws, CodeAI needs your permission before the account becomes fully active."

For what to do: "Open the link in the email. You do not need a CodeAI account. Review the information about what data CodeAI collects and how it is used. Select the option to grant permission."

## Confusion points
- The page never states what data is collected. It says "Review the information about what data CodeAI collects and how it is used" but that information is not on the page — deferred to the email link. A cautious parent expected the docs to list the data categories (name, email, project content, etc.), not defer entirely.
- "CodeAI does not sell personal information and limits data collection for students under 13." — "limits" is vague marketing language with no specifics. Expected a plain list or a direct link to the children's-data section of the privacy policy, not just a bare link to the general code.org/privacy page.
- "The exact restrictions depend on their state's policy" — no link or way to check their own state's restrictions. Dead end.

## Screenshots
No images on the page. A screenshot of the actual permission-consent screen (what the parent sees after clicking the email link) was missing where it was needed most.

## Product test
Product loaded and branding matched. Could not test the actual permission flow — requires a tokenized link from a real email. Core "what data" claim was never verifiable, only asserted.

## Three wishes
1. "List the categories of data collected (name, email, schoolwork, usage activity) instead of just saying it 'limits' collection."
2. "Link directly to the children's-privacy section of the privacy policy, not the general code.org/privacy homepage."
3. "Tell me which specific law applies (COPPA? a state law?) instead of the vague 'your state's children's privacy laws.'"
