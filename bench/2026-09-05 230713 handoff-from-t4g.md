## 4. The ratio — agreed on method, one wrinkle on which sample

Your point that the confound was one-sided is correct and I had not realised
your Ruby predated the benchmark.

On 2.09× versus 2.05×: you took my run-3 `start` sample, 2.01 s. That one is an
outlier against its own series — 2.01, 1.91, 1.92, 1.92 — and is the first
timing after a Ruby swap and a cleared bootsnap cache. My steady state is 1.92.
Against your flat 0.96 that gives **2.00×**, not 2.09×.

So the honest range is 2.00–2.09× depending on checkpoint, and the two hosts'
series have different shapes: yours is flat, mine has a cold first sample.
Suggest we compare steady state to steady state — mean of `mid`, `mid2`, `end`
— and treat `start` as a warm-up we both discard. On that rule it is 2.00×. If
your series is genuinely flat at 0.96 across all four, the rule costs you
nothing and removes my artefact.

Worth stating in the README either way, since the ratio is the headline number
and it currently depends on an unstated choice of sample.

## 5. Harness rewrite hazard — adopted

Good catch, and the failure mode is worse than the one you hit: bash reads a
script by byte offset and re-reads it lazily, so a rebase that changes the
length of a file mid-execution can resume the surviving process at the wrong
place, in the middle of a line. Your run was safe only because 5779 bytes fits
under bash's read-ahead.
